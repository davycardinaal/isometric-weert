# Isometric Weert

A zoomable isometric pixel-art map of Weert (Limburg, the Netherlands): 3 × 3 km around the Sint-Martinuskerk, with the old town inside the singels, the station, the Zuid-Willemsvaart and the neighbourhoods around them. It is rendered procedurally from Dutch open data, so it is exact, seamless and rebuilds in a few minutes.

Made with the code and the prompt of [Isometric Helenaveen](https://github.com/jankeesvw/isometric-helenaveen) by [@jankeesvw](https://x.com/intent/follow?screen_name=jankeesvw), who rebuilt his village in isometric pixel art with Claude Code and nothing but open data, in the spirit of [isometric.nyc](https://cannoneyed.com/projects/isometric-nyc). This repository is that pipeline, pointed at Weert.

## Pipeline

Run `./build.sh` for everything. The steps:

1. `scripts/fetch.py` downloads the raw data into `data/raw/`: 3D BAG buildings (LoD2.2 with roof shapes, fetched per 500 m chunk because a town has some 17,000 of them), BGT surfaces (roads, water, plots, vegetation), the PDOK aerial photo at 25 cm and AHN surface and terrain heights at 50 cm.
2. `scripts/landmarks.py` names the places people look for: the Sint-Martinuskerk, the castle of the counts of Horne, the monasteries, the station, the windmills, the bridges over the canal, monuments and murals, each with a short story written from the Dutch Wikipedia. It can also give a building its own colors and add what 3D BAG smooths away, like a tower or a roof turret measured in AHN (`tower`, `turret`, `bell`), and it builds the windmills from their own measures instead of the straight prisms 3D BAG makes of them, the Wilhelmus-Hubertus on its belt with sails that the viewer turns (`mill`). `scripts/build_buildings.py` triangulates the 3D BAG solids and lays the aerial photo on every roof (`scripts/roof_atlas.py`): the photo is lined up with each footprint by matching edges (it is off by 1.2 m on average), each roof face is evened out so the photo's sun and shade don't double up with the renderer's lighting, and the result is reduced to a few flat colors so solar panels, skylights and chimneys show as clean shapes. 3D BAG cannot reconstruct greenhouses (lidar goes through glass), so those are extruded from their footprint with a gutter height based on the year of construction. Water tanks, feed silos and sheds come from the BGT.
3. `scripts/build_trees.py` finds individual trees in the canopy height model (AHN surface minus terrain): local maxima for the tops, watershed segmentation for crown size, and the spring aerial photo to tell evergreens from deciduous trees. Municipal street trees from the BGT fill in what the lidar missed.
4. `scripts/build_ground.py` paints the BGT polygons, plus football and tennis lines, the riding arenas and both graveyards from OpenStreetMap, in a fixed palette with patterns (furrows along each field's long axis, paving grids, ripples, a dark far bank on every ditch) and bakes hard sun shadows of the buildings and tree crowns into it.
5. `render/scene.js` is a three.js scene with a 2:1 isometric orthographic camera. Window grids, brick plinths, metal cladding and greenhouse glass are drawn in the building shader, a post pass smooths the image with a Kuwahara filter (a painterly look without grain) and inks every depth step as an outline. Tree crowns are cel-shaded with three tones. `render/run.mjs` renders it in headless Chromium on the GPU (Vulkan), tile by tile with a view offset, so tiles line up exactly.
6. `vips dzsave` cuts the stitched image into a Deep Zoom pyramid of WebP tiles, and `viewer/index.html` shows it with OpenSeadragon: tilt-shift blur, GPS coordinates under the cursor, a search box over every place and address (`scripts/build_places.py`), named buildings and places that light up on hover and open a card with their story, photo and links when clicked, grouped into series (De graven van Horne, Kerken en kloosters, Langs het kanaal, Molens, Oorlog en herdenking, Kunst) to step through like the tour (`scripts/landmarks.py`, `scripts/build_hotspots.py`; a link ending in `#horne` opens a series), a guided tour "Weert van toen" along fifteen historical places with photos from Wikimedia Commons, each linking to the Wikipedia article it was written from (`scripts/build_history.py`; a link ending in `#van-toen` starts it), and a toggle for the walking routes and numbered junctions of the wandelknooppuntennetwerk from OpenStreetMap (`scripts/build_routes.py`). Little cars, vans, trucks and cyclists drive the OpenStreetMap roads and cycle paths on the right-hand side, keeping their distance and passing behind buildings and under tree crowns: the renderer also writes a mask of everything that stands more than 30 cm above the ground (`--pass objects`), `site.sh` turns the map under that mask into a second, transparent tile layer, and the viewer draws whatever is behind something between the two layers, and now and then geese cross high up in a V (`scripts/build_traffic.py`). A link ending in `#Sint-Martinuskerk` or `#Station-Weert` opens on that place. A compass button turns the map a quarter: the map is rendered from all four diagonal directions (`VIEWS` in `scripts/config.py`, `render/run.mjs --look`), and the viewer turns a still of the old view about the middle of the screen like a turntable while the new view turns in after it, keeping the spot in the middle in place; halfway through the turn the pixels grow coarse and fine again, like an old game console. The map opens on the Sint-Martinuskerk, and the home button goes back there. A music button plays [My Street](https://opengameart.org/content/my-street) by congusbongus (CC0, from OpenGameArt) on a loop; it is off until a visitor turns it on, and the browser remembers that for next time. A moon button turns the map to evening (`viewer/og.png`, the picture for link previews, shows that evening): an SVG colour matrix (`#dusk`) puts the map, its traffic and the minimap in moonlight, and a canvas above it lights a lamp every 34 m along the streets and the headlights and rear lights of every car and bike; it is remembered the same way. A click on the map, not on a place, drops a pin and writes it into the address with the zoom and direction (`?pin=51.25361,5.70694&zoom=1.23&view=135`), so that link opens on the same spot. The viewer speaks Dutch and English, after the browser's language (`?lang=en` or `?lang=nl` asks for one); static text carries its English in `data-en*` attributes, and the stories, kinds and photo credits from the data files are looked up by their Dutch in `viewer/en.json`. A button in the corner opens the prompt this map was made with, to make one of another village or town.

`./site.sh` cuts the rendered image into tiles and assembles `site/` without rendering anything; each run writes its tiles to a fresh `tiles-<stamp>/` folder so browsers never mix cached tiles of two versions. `./deploy.sh` publishes `site/` to the `gh-pages` branch as a single fresh commit, so the tiles never pile up in the history of `main`.

The camera looks north-east and the sun comes from the west-south-west (`scripts/sun.py`, mirrored in `render/scene.js`).

Overlays (search, hover outlines, walking routes, the tour, traffic) are JSON files next to the tiles: changing them needs no rendering, only the small build script and `./deploy.sh`.

## Iterating

`node render/run.mjs crop <x> <y> <w> <h> --scale 4 --out out/crop.png` renders one region of the full image in a second, which is the fastest way to try a shader or palette change. `node render/run.mjs crop 0 0 1697 869 --scale 0.4` gives an overview of the whole map.

## Data

3D BAG (TU Delft, 3DGI, CC BY 4.0), BGT (CC0), AHN (CC0), BAG addresses and the aerial photo (Beeldmateriaal Nederland, CC BY 4.0), all via [PDOK](https://www.pdok.nl). Points of interest, pitches, graveyards, roads and walking routes from OpenStreetMap (ODbL). Places and their coordinates from Wikidata (CC0); the stories are written from the Dutch Wikipedia (CC BY-SA 4.0) and link to their article. Photos from Wikimedia Commons, each with its author and license in the caption. Music: My Street by congusbongus (CC0).
